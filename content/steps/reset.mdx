## Should you take a break after being laid off?

Yes, a short one. Before you update a single bullet or message a single person, take a day or two off. A week at most. Visit a friend. Go somewhere that isn't your apartment. Do something that changes your mood, whatever that is for you.

This is coaching judgment, not research. I've watched a lot of people start their search the morning after a layoff, and the ones who took a short, deliberate break almost always ran a better process than the ones who didn't.

## Why the break is worth it

**The search will take months.** A few days off at the start costs you almost nothing. The first week of a search is also the least productive one, because you haven't done the thinking in [step two](/story/) yet. Most people spend it firing off applications that go nowhere.

**You won't do your best work while you're angry.** Layoffs are usually a business decision made in a spreadsheet by people who never met you. That doesn't make them hurt less. If you start calling people while you're still processing it, it leaks into everything: the way you describe your last job, the story you tell about yourself, the tone of your outreach. Your first conversations are with the people who like you most, and you only get to have them once.

Bart Jaworski saw his layoff from Microsoft coming, and it still hit him with self-doubt and anger. He describes realizing he was "just a row in the Excel." Instead of throwing himself straight back into work, he gave himself room to process: a trip he'd already planned, a few fun side projects. He still landed a new role quickly.

**You want to come out motivated.** The goal of the reset is to start the search determined to run a tight process, not reeling from the layoff.

## Cap it

The risk with "take some time" is that it turns into a month. That's a procrastination permit, and the longer it runs, the harder the restart.

So pick your start date now and put it on your calendar. Tell one person what it is. When the date arrives, open [step two](/story/) and start.

Once you're back, momentum matters more than it seems. Anneli Scopazzi, a recruiter who has worked at Figma, Notion and Palantir, tells candidates to use the time between interviews to build or ship something small. It gives you stories to tell, and you walk into conversations excited about what you're learning instead of sounding only urgent.

## What to do first after being laid off

Mostly: rest, see people, move your body, sleep. The first practical steps after a layoff are a few admin tasks that have deadlines or get harder with time:

- **File for unemployment** if you're eligible. Rules and waiting periods vary by state and country, and benefits generally run from when you file, so don't sit on it.
- **Read your severance agreement before you sign it.** There's usually a deadline, and it's worth knowing what you're agreeing to. If the stakes are high, an hour with an employment lawyer is cheap.
- **Sort out health insurance** and any other benefits with cutoff dates. In the US, that usually means comparing COBRA (keeping your employer's plan, at full price) with a marketplace plan. Losing job-based coverage lets you enroll in a marketplace plan outside the usual window, but only for a limited time.
- **Ask for references while goodwill is high.** Your manager and close teammates feel bad right now and want to help. Ask whether they'd be a reference, and ask for a short LinkedIn recommendation while your work is fresh in their minds.
- **Save what you're allowed to keep.** Personal contacts, your own performance reviews, public work samples. Not company confidential material.

## How to explain being laid off

You'll be asked about it in almost every conversation, including "tell me about yourself" and "why are you looking?" in interviews, so settle on one calm sentence now. Layoffs are common, and nobody serious holds one against you. What they notice is how you talk about it.

<Example label="How to explain being laid off in an interview">

"My role was eliminated in a company-wide reduction in March. I'm using it as a chance to focus on [the thing you want next], which is why I wanted to talk to you."

</Example>

Don't badmouth the company or your manager, even if they deserve it. Don't over-explain. Say the sentence and move to what you want next.

Not every layoff is random, and it's worth being honest with yourself about yours. Ethan Evans, a retired Amazon VP, was laid off twice early in his career. He says both were earned: he was abrasive and critical of his peers, and as a manager today he'd make the same call. His point is the one that matters for you. Whatever the reason, you recover.

If there's a real lesson in your layoff, take it into the next job. You don't owe anyone that story in your one sentence.

## Questions people ask after a layoff

### How long does it take to find a job after being laid off?

Longer than most people expect. In mid-2026 the median stretch of unemployment in the US was about 11 weeks, and roughly a quarter of unemployed people had been looking for six months or more ([Bureau of Labor Statistics](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.nr0.htm)). Senior roles usually take longer. Plan your money and your energy for months, which is exactly why a few days off at the start costs so little.

### Is a layoff the same as being fired?

No. A layoff means the company eliminated your role for business reasons. Being fired means you were let go for performance or conduct. Say "I was laid off" or "my role was eliminated," and don't let anyone blur the two, including you.

### Should you put "laid off" on your resume?

No. Your resume lists dates, not reasons. Explain the layoff in one sentence when someone asks, and use the resume for what you accomplished.

### Should you post about your layoff on LinkedIn?

It can work well, but post after you've done [step two](/story/), not the day it happens. A post that says what you're looking for and what you're good at gives people something to act on. A post that only announces the layoff gets sympathy and very few leads.

### Can you negotiate your severance?

Sometimes. It's reasonable to ask what's flexible: extra weeks of pay, extended health coverage, accelerated vesting, the wording of your departure and an agreed reference. Whether you can collect unemployment while receiving severance depends on your state, so check with your state's unemployment office before you assume either way.

<Checklist>

- You've picked a start date for the search, and it's within a week.
- Unemployment, severance and insurance deadlines are written down.
- You've asked two or three people to be references.
- You have one calm sentence that explains the layoff.

</Checklist>
